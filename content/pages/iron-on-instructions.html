How to iron on your patches

A heat press gives the strongest, longest-lasting bond. Use the settings below for each patch type, then let the patch cool completely before you move the garment.

Heat press settings
Patch typeTemperatureTime
Embroidered160°C / 320°F15–20 s
Chenille160°C / 320°F15–20 s
PVC160°C / 320°F20–30 s
Printed (sublimated)160°C / 320°F15 s
Leather140°C / 284°F15–20 s
Silicone labels160°C / 320°F15 s

Embroidered

  1. Place the patch on the garment.
  2. Press with a heat press.
  3. Let it cool before handling.

Chenille

  1. Spray fabric adhesive on the back of the patch.
  2. Place the patch on the garment.
  3. Put a towel or fuzzy material on the press surface.
  4. Lay the garment patch-side down and press.

PVC

  1. Spray fabric adhesive on the back of the patch.
  2. Place the patch on the garment.
  3. Lay the garment patch-side down and press. Never put an iron directly on PVC.

Printed (sublimated)

  1. Place the patch on the garment.
  2. Press with a heat press.
  3. Let it cool before handling.

Leather

  1. Spray fabric adhesive on the back of the patch.
  2. Place the patch on the garment.
  3. Lay the garment patch-side down and press. Leather is damaged above 200°C.

Silicone labels

  1. Position the label.
  2. Cover with a clean cloth.
  3. Press firmly and let it cool.

Using a household iron

Set the iron to the hottest setting your fabric allows and turn off the steam. Place the patch, cover it with a thin cloth, and press down firmly for 20–30 seconds without sliding the iron. Turn the garment inside out and press the back for another 20–30 seconds.

Never put an iron directly on PVC, sequin or silicone patches. For denim, leather and heavy jackets, stitch the edges after ironing for a permanent hold.

Care

Wait 24 hours before the first wash. Wash inside out in cold water, skip bleach, and air dry or tumble on low. If an edge starts to lift, re-press it or add a few stitches.

In an Arizona summer, do not leave freshly applied patches in a closed car for the first day; extreme heat can soften adhesive before it fully sets.
