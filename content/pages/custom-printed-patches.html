When the design is a sunset

Some Arizona designs are made of gradients: a Sonoran sunset, a desert landscape photo, a car-show poster, a memorial portrait. Embroidery would flatten those into a few blocks of color. Sublimation keeps every shade.

Printed patches are also the fastest way to turn existing artwork into merch for a fundraiser, a family reunion or a one-weekend event, because there is no thread count to manage.

Borders

Merrow border

Our recommendation for sublimated designs. Stops fraying and frames the print.

Embroidered border

A satin-stitched frame in any thickness. Works on custom die-cut shapes where a merrow edge cannot follow.

No border

Clean, sharp edges for a modern look. Better for short-term use.

Backings

Sew-on

Recommended for clothes you wear often.

Heat-activated adhesive for a near-permanent bond that lasts 50+ washes when applied with a heat press.

Hook side on the patch, loop side on the gear. Swap patches in seconds on uniforms, packs and caps.

Peel & stick

Temporary adhesive for events, costumes and one-day wear. No sewing or heat.

Questions

Printed Patches FAQ

Can you make a patch from a photo?

Yes. Photos, logos and personal images all work. Send the highest resolution file you have.

How are printed patches made?

With dye sublimation. Digital ink is heat-transferred into polyester twill so the design becomes part of the fabric.

Does a printed patch need a border?

We recommend one. A merrow or embroidered border prevents fraying and protects the edge.

Can I use very detailed artwork?

Yes, but avoid tiny lettering; text should stay large enough to read at the patch size.

Can printed patches be washed?

Yes. Wash gently and cold to keep colors bright.

Printed or woven?

Woven patches build the image from fine thread with no base fabric. Printed patches put full-color images on twill, which handles photos and gradients best.
