Light enough for a July tee

A thick embroidered patch on a lightweight summer shirt can feel stiff and hot. Woven patches lie flat and stay flexible, which is why Arizona apparel brands and boutiques use them for hem tags, sleeve hits and hat fronts that get worn every day.

They are also the right choice when your logo has fine print, like an address line, a small "Est." date or a detailed mark that would fill in if stitched.

Borders

Embroidered border

A satin-stitched frame in any thickness. Works on custom die-cut shapes where a merrow edge cannot follow.

Merrow border

A thick overlocked edge that stops fraying. Best on circles, ovals, squares and other simple shapes.

No border

A raw, clean edge for a modern or DIY look.

Backings

Sew-on

Recommended. The most secure hold for a thin woven patch.

Iron-on

Iron-on with a paper backing gives the best feel and works well on light garments. Not ideal for very heavy woven patches.

Velcro (hook & loop)

Hook side on the patch, loop side on the gear. Swap patches in seconds on uniforms, packs and caps.

Peel & stick

Possible for temporary use, but not recommended for woven.

Questions

Woven Patches FAQ

How are woven patches made?

On a jacquard loom that interlaces polyester or cotton threads into the design, instead of stitching thread onto a base.

Are woven patches good for small designs?

Yes. The finer thread keeps small text and thin details readable.

Woven or embroidered?

Woven is thinner, sleeker and holds more detail. Embroidered is thicker, more textured and generally the more rugged of the two.

Can I wash them?

Yes. Wash the garment gently; hand washing keeps woven patches looking new the longest.

Which backing do you recommend?

Sew-on for the strongest hold, or iron-on with paper backing for a clean feel on light garments.
